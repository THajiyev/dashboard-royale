How are move estimates calculated?

Our location model was fit on Jan–Apr machine-day data. It models coin-in per available hour with a log link, adjusting for game theme, weekday, events, holidays and the Back Forty closure. Each bank coefficient estimates its effect relative to the model's reference bank; converting it with exp(coefficient) − 1 gives the estimated percentage difference. These are adjusted associations, not proof that location alone caused the difference.

For a move, we compare the destination and original bank effects, apply that difference to the machine's average daily theo over the selected period, then project 28 days in July after a 2-day lead time. A swap counts both machines. The plan subtracts the move-cost setting (default $1,800 per machine) and three hours of estimated lost theo per move. The 90% range reflects uncertainty in the bank coefficients; it does not capture all the uncertainty of a real move. This is projected theo, not guaranteed profit or actual cash revenue. High-Limit and B-51 effects are not attributed to location.

When to stop moving machines

The optimizer adds moves one step at a time, best value per machine first, using the same projection as dragging. A step is a machine into an empty slot or a swap of two. Each extra move is worth making only while it earns back its price over July's 28 days. The catalogue allows at most 24 machine moves. Bank B-51 is left out: its eight machines went in on Mar 24, so its high effect belongs to them, not the spot.
