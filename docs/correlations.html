What moves together

Four correlation matrices, each answering a question the other pages depend on. Blue is positive and red negative. A grey cell isn't significant once every pair in its matrix is corrected for multiple testing (Benjamini–Hochberg, q ≥ 0.05). Hover a cell for r, p, q and n.

Daily floor metrics

One row per gaming day, Jan–Jun. Raw daily numbers all rise and fall with the weekday cycle, so they mostly re-measure the calendar. Removing the weekday and the slow level leaves what actually co-moves from one day to the next.

What it says

    Machines: what persists from Jan–Apr to May–Jun

    One row per machine on the floor in both periods. Spearman rank correlation. This is the test behind the Machines page: a cut should follow what persists, not what was luck.

    What it says

      Carded players: habits persist, luck doesn't

      One row per carded player seen in both Jan–Mar and Apr–Jun. Spearman rank correlation. Luck is the player's result minus theo, divided by theo: positive means they won more than the game math expected.

      What it says

        Forecast errors: why combining models didn't help

        Daily errors (forecast minus actual) on May 1 – Jun 30 for the main models from the Models page. Averaging forecasts only helps when their errors disagree.

        What it says

          How these were computed

          • Daily, weekday and trend removed: ordinary days only. Money and count measures are logged; each loses its weekday mean, then a centred 15-day rolling median. Temperature is taken against its own 15-day mean (hotter than recent days); rain is left in millimetres. This is the same recipe as the weather check (WEATHER_NOTE.md).
          • Machines and players: Spearman rank correlation, because money per machine and per player is heavily skewed. Luck z is a machine's house win minus theo over its own game-math standard deviation.
          • p and q: q is the Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction over every pair in that matrix. Daily series are autocorrelated, so their p-values are optimistic; read them as rankings of evidence, not exact error rates.
          • Correlation isn't cause. The player matrix shows a case where a strong raw correlation disappears once play volume is held fixed.
