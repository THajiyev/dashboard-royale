What did the loyalty (comp) program cost, and did anything about it change?

Comps

Comps cost $191K over six months. On May 1 the casino halved the points a comp costs. That raised the bill 9% and brought in no extra play.

Points needed for $1 of comps

Median per gaming day, by the player's tier at redemption.

What happened on May 1

The comp redemption rate was changed floor-wide, at the start of the May 1 gaming day. This is the same lever as the catalogue's "change comp redemption rate floor-wide" action, set from 15 to 7, without a control group.

The evidence

    Did it bring in play?

    Local card holders' theo, indexed to their Mar–Apr average. Visitors almost never return, so they can't be compared this way.

    Before and after May 1

    Comps got half as expensive in points, but guests didn't redeem much more often or for bigger items. They spent fewer points and kept the rest.

    Tiers

    Higher tiers pay fewer points per comp and get more back per dollar of expected loss. Platinum and Gold grew every month, which raises the program's cost even before the price change.

    Month by month

    Reinvestment is the comps' real cost divided by tracked theo, the expected loss of the same card holders.

    The answer

      Confirming it: a pilot in August

      Next in line for August: put a random half of card holders back on the old prices for 30 days.

      1. Groups. Random half of active card holders, stratified by tier and home region.
      2. Measure. Theo per card holder and visits per card holder, test vs control, plus comp cost per dollar of theo.
      3. Decide. If the old-price half plays as much, restore the old rate for everyone.

      A floor-wide rate change ($1,500) can't be combined with the pilot, and changing prices again without a control group repeats May's problem: no way to tell what it did. Open the action plan
