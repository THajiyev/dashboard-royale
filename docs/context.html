What else moves play

The competing explanations we tested: access, events, weather and the city's own tourism trend. Access and events change where and how much people play. Weather mostly doesn't, apart from rain keeping locals home.

Back Forty lost its front door for five weeks

The walkway from the main entrance closed for renovation Mar 3 – Apr 6. Weekly gap between Back Forty and five control zones, coin-in per hour, with 90% intervals. Week 0 is the closure.

Walkway closed 90% interval

Placebo zones, each treated as if closed, moved between −2% and +3%. The whole floor, measured directly, was +1.6% (90% CI −0.2% to +3.4%): players walked to other zones instead of leaving. Access decides where people play more than whether they play. Source: outputs/model_closure_event_study.csv.

Effect on play, after weekday and fortnight

Play is measured against what is normal for that weekday and that fortnight, so seasons aren't mistaken for weather. 148 ordinary days, Newey–West errors.

The rain effect passed a placebo test: 4 random days never came close (p < 0.001), and it shows in both rain episodes, Feb 13 and May 4–6. All nine 40°C days fall in June's tourism slump, so heat can't be separated from it. Even a perfect weather forecast would have cut the May–Jun error by about 5%. Weather belongs on the dashboard as an alert, not in the forecast. Source: analysis/WEATHER_NOTE.md.
