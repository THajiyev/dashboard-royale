How unusual were the results?

A p-value per player for two questions, and the spread of those p-values across all players. If play is fair, p-values are spread evenly, so every band sits near the flat line; a pile-up near 0 means more unusual results than chance gives.

  1. Win rate. How often their spins paid anything, against each game's published hit probability. Exact binomial test, then Benjamini–Hochberg correction for testing every player.
  2. Payout. Actual against expected payout. Jackpots make slot payouts lopsided, so the bell curve would call thousands of fair players anomalous. The p-value is empirical instead: twice the share of players on the same game with a similar number of spins whose result was at least as extreme.
