Models and validation

Every forecast was trained on Jan 1 – Apr 30 and scored once on May 1 – Jun 30. Only models chosen before May count as honest wins; the rest are shown so you can see what hindsight would have picked.

The test, step by step

  1. Split by time. Jan 1 – Apr 30 (120 days) to learn, May 1 – Jun 30 (61 days) to test. No random split, so no future day leaks into training.
  2. Build the baseline. B1 is the Jan–Apr average theo for each day of the week, the chart on the right. No events, holidays or outside data.
  3. Choose in advance. Candidates were ranked by rolling-origin cross-validation inside Jan–Apr. The pick was recorded before May was opened.
  4. Freeze. A fixed forecast is made once on Apr 30 for all 61 days. A refresh model refits every 7 days using only data up to that day.
  5. Score once. RMSE is the main metric, MAE alongside. The 95% CI on the change vs B1 comes from a 7-day moving-block bootstrap, 2,000 resamples.
  6. Make intervals honestly. 90% bands are split-conformal: quantiles of each model's Jan–Apr cross-validation errors, applied to the frozen forecast.

The numbers behind it

Event days are EDC (May 16–18) and Locals Week (Jun 9–15). The 90% band should catch about 55 of 61 days; most catch fewer, so the bands are too narrow.

Six rounds of trying to beat it

    How close to the limit

    Ordinary-day RMSE of fits that were allowed to see the answers. If hindsight can only do this well, a forecast made weeks ahead has little left to gain without new information, such as hotel bookings.

    Source: analysis/experiments/iter5/noise_floor.csv

    Classical and machine-learning models

    The first round, scored on MAE. The model cross-validation picked (LightGBM) came 7th on the holdout: two folds of Jan–Apr were too noisy to choose between models whose errors differ by about $500 a day.

    Machine model: is it the machine or the spot?

    Coin-in per available hour for each machine-day in May–Jun, from models fit on Jan–Apr. Knowing a machine's bank and game beats knowing its own history.

    Machine effects after location and game are small (spread τ ≈ 1.7%) and barely persist into May–Jun (Spearman 0.27). None of the GM's bottom 10 has a credibly negative machine effect. Source: analysis/models.md, Phase 3.

    Change-impact estimator, checked on fake changes

    Before reading any real result, 150 fake changes were planted at random units and dates, with no effect and with −15% or +10% injected. Intervals widened by ×1.25 (fit on one seed, confirmed on another).

    A single machine's change has to be large to show: +10% is caught a third of the time. Source: analysis/impact.md.
